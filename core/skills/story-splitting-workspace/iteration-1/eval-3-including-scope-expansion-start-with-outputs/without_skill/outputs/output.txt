USER STORY ANALYSIS
===================

Story: "Generate a monthly financial report including revenue breakdown, expense categories, and year-over-year comparison charts"

VERDICT: This story is too big. Split it.

WHY IT IS TOO BIG
-----------------

The word "including" is the key signal here. It introduces a list of three
distinct deliverables, each with its own data requirements, business logic,
and UI complexity:

1. Revenue breakdown - requires fetching and aggregating revenue data by
   source, product, or business unit for a given month.

2. Expense categories - requires fetching and aggregating expense data
   grouped by category for the same period.

3. Year-over-year comparison charts - requires fetching data for the prior
   year's equivalent period, computing deltas, and rendering charts.

On top of that, "generate a monthly financial report" bundles together:
- Data retrieval and aggregation
- Report formatting/layout
- Output format (PDF? dashboard? email? download?)

Each of those three sections could fail, be delayed, or need redesign
independently of the others. Keeping them as one story makes it impossible
to deliver incrementally and hard to estimate accurately.

A reasonable rule of thumb: if a story requires work across multiple
backend aggregations AND a visualization layer, it is almost certainly
too big to complete in a single sprint iteration.

PROPOSED SPLIT
--------------

Story 1: Display monthly revenue breakdown
  As a finance user, I want to see this month's revenue broken down by
  [source/category], so I can understand where income is coming from.

  Acceptance criteria:
  - Revenue totals are aggregated and displayed for the selected month
  - Breakdown shows revenue by the agreed dimension (product line, channel, etc.)
  - No charts required; a table is sufficient

  Why split here: Revenue data has its own data model, queries, and
  display requirements. It can be delivered and validated independently.

Story 2: Display monthly expense categories
  As a finance user, I want to see this month's expenses grouped by
  category, so I can understand where money is being spent.

  Acceptance criteria:
  - Expenses are aggregated and displayed for the selected month
  - Grouped by category
  - No charts required; a table is sufficient

  Why split here: Expense data comes from a different source/model than
  revenue. Separating it avoids blocking both on a single piece of work.

Story 3: Add year-over-year comparison to the financial report
  As a finance user, I want to compare this month's revenue and expenses
  against the same month last year, so I can identify trends.

  Acceptance criteria:
  - Prior-year data is fetched for the equivalent month
  - Delta (absolute and percentage) is shown alongside current values
  - At minimum a table view; charts are a stretch goal for this story

  Why split here: YoY comparison depends on stories 1 and 2 being done
  first (or at least the data model being stable). It also introduces
  a new data retrieval concern (historical period queries).

Story 4: Add charts to the financial report
  As a finance user, I want to see visual charts for revenue, expenses,
  and year-over-year trends, so I can quickly spot patterns.

  Acceptance criteria:
  - Charts are rendered for the data already available in the report
  - At minimum: revenue chart, expense chart, YoY comparison chart
  - Charts update when the selected month changes

  Why split here: Charts are a presentation layer concern that can be
  added on top of working tabular data. Delaying charts unblocks earlier
  delivery of business value and keeps visualization complexity isolated.

SEQUENCING RECOMMENDATION
--------------------------

The natural order is 1 → 2 → 3 → 4, because:
- Stories 1 and 2 are independent and can be done in parallel if the
  team has capacity.
- Story 3 depends on having stable revenue and expense data (stories 1+2).
- Story 4 depends on story 3 (needs all data including YoY deltas to
  render meaningful charts).

Each story is independently releasable and delivers visible value to
the finance user without requiring the others to be complete first.
