STORY SPLITTING ANALYSIS
========================

Original Story:
"Generate a monthly financial report including revenue breakdown, expense categories, and year-over-year comparison charts"

---

RED FLAGS DETECTED
==================

Scanning for linguistic indicators...

1. "INCLUDING" (Scope Indicator) - The word "including" is a direct signal of feature bundling.
   It introduces three separate outputs bundled into one story:
   - revenue breakdown
   - expense categories
   - year-over-year comparison charts

2. "AND" (Coordinating Conjunction) - Appears between "expense categories" and "year-over-year comparison charts",
   meaning at least two independent outputs are being requested simultaneously.

3. COMPLEX OUTPUT (Reports + Charts) - Multiple output types are bundled together:
   - Tabular data (revenue breakdown)
   - Tabular data (expense categories)
   - Visual output (charts with year-over-year comparison)

VERDICT: This story is TOO BIG. It bundles at least 3 distinct deliverables into a single story.

---

DECISION TREE APPLICATION
===========================

Red flags mapped to recommended techniques:

| Red Flag         | Problem                          | Recommended Technique                     |
|------------------|----------------------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| "including"      | Feature bundling / scope expansion | #3 Extract basic utility first            |
| "and"            | Multiple independent outputs     | Split by conjunction                      |
| Complex output   | Too many outputs at once         | #1 Start with outputs (incrementally)     |
| Charts           | Visual output = richer, riskier  | #5 Simplify outputs (text before charts)  |

Multiple red flags detected => apply techniques sequentially.

---

SPLITTING PROCESS
=================

Step 1: Split by "including" (remove bundled outputs)

"Generate a monthly financial report including [X, Y, and Z]" means we're shipping
three outputs at once. Each output should be its own story.

The three outputs are:
  A. Revenue breakdown
  B. Expense categories
  C. Year-over-year comparison charts

Step 2: Split by "and" (further separate independent items)

Even if we separate charts from the rest, "revenue breakdown" and "expense categories"
are two distinct data views. Neither requires the other to deliver value.

Step 3: Apply "Start with outputs" + "Simplify outputs"

Charts are the most complex output (require data aggregation, visualization library,
rendering). Text/tabular summaries come first. Charts come later only when base data
is validated.

Step 4: Extract basic utility first

Before generating any breakdown, we should validate that a basic report shell exists:
- Does a report page/document exist?
- Does it show a time period (month/year)?
- Does it show at least one number?

That's the walking skeleton. Add breakdowns on top.

---

PROPOSED SPLITS
===============

Story 1: "Generate a basic monthly report showing total revenue for a given month"
  - Why first? Proves the report shell works end-to-end
  - Output: Single number (total revenue for the selected month)
  - No breakdown, no charts, no expense data
  - Time: 2-4 hours
  - Value: Stakeholders can verify the report exists and numbers are correct
  - Deployable alone: YES

Story 2: "Add revenue breakdown by category/product line to the monthly report"
  - Builds on Story 1's report shell
  - Output: Table or list showing revenue per category
  - No charts yet, no expense data
  - Time: 3-5 hours
  - Value: Finance team can see where revenue comes from
  - Deployable alone: YES (Story 1 ships first, Story 2 enriches it)

Story 3: "Add expense categories summary to the monthly report"
  - Builds on Story 1's report shell
  - Output: Table or list showing expenses per category
  - Independent from Story 2 (revenue breakdown)
  - Time: 3-5 hours
  - Value: Finance team can see where money is being spent
  - Deployable alone: YES

Story 4: "Add year-over-year comparison as a text/table summary"
  - Compare current month vs same month last year (numbers only, no charts yet)
  - Output: Simple table: "Revenue this month vs same month last year: +12%"
  - No chart rendering required
  - Time: 3-4 hours
  - Value: Leadership can see trend direction without charts
  - Deployable alone: YES

Story 5: "Add year-over-year comparison charts to the monthly report"
  - Builds on Story 4's data (chart is just a visual wrapper)
  - Output: Bar chart or line chart showing month vs prior year
  - Time: 4-6 hours
  - Value: Visual representation for presentations/dashboards
  - Deployable alone: YES (only after Story 4 validates the data)

---

SMALLEST SLICE TO SHIP FIRST
==============================

Story 1: "Generate a basic monthly report showing total revenue for a given month"

Why this is the right first slice:
- Takes 2-4 hours
- Delivers immediate, verifiable value (total revenue number)
- Proves the data pipeline works end-to-end
- Stakeholders can validate correctness before adding complexity
- Everything else builds on top of this foundation
- If the total revenue number is wrong, we find out NOW - not after building 5 stories

Shipping Story 1 alone is production-worthy and useful. The rest are enhancements.

---

RECOMMENDED DELIVERY ORDER
============================

Sprint / Week 1:
  - Story 1: Basic monthly report with total revenue   [2-4h]
  - Story 3: Expense categories summary               [3-5h]

Sprint / Week 2:
  - Story 2: Revenue breakdown by category            [3-5h]
  - Story 4: Year-over-year comparison (text/table)   [3-4h]

Sprint / Week 3 (only if needed):
  - Story 5: Year-over-year comparison charts         [4-6h]

Total estimated time: 15-24 hours spread across 3 sprints.
Original bundled story would have been estimated as 1-2 weeks of work, likely
delivered all at once with higher integration risk.

---

TECHNIQUES APPLIED
===================

- Split by "including"   -> Removed 3 bundled outputs into 3 separate stories
- Split by "and"         -> Separated revenue breakdown from expense categories
- Start with outputs     -> Delivered simplest output (total number) first
- Simplify outputs       -> Text/table year-over-year before charts
- Extract basic utility  -> Report shell (Story 1) before any breakdown

COACHING NOTES
===============

Challenge questions to ask the team:

- "If you had to ship something by end of day tomorrow, what would it be?"
  -> Answer: Story 1 (total revenue number). That's your first story.

- "What's the worst that could happen if we ship just the revenue total first?"
  -> Almost nothing. Finance can start validating data quality immediately.

- "Do we actually need charts, or would a table be enough for now?"
  -> Ask stakeholders. Charts add complexity (visualization library, rendering,
     responsive design). A table might meet 90% of the need.

- "Can we avoid doing the year-over-year comparison entirely until someone asks for it?"
  -> Yes. Ship Stories 1-3 first. Add Story 4 and 5 only when there is proven demand.
