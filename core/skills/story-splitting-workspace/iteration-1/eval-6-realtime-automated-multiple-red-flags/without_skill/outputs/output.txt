STORY ANALYSIS
==============

Original story:
"Build a real-time notification system that automatically pushes alerts to users via email, SMS, and in-app messages based on their notification preferences"

VERDICT: This story is too big. It should be split.

WHY IT IS TOO BIG
-----------------

This story bundles at least five distinct concerns into one unit of work:

1. Three separate delivery channels (email, SMS, in-app) - each has its own
   third-party service, error handling, retry logic, and testing surface.

2. A real-time push mechanism - this implies WebSockets, SSE, or a push
   notification service, which is a non-trivial infrastructure concern on its own.

3. A notification preference model - storing, reading, and enforcing per-user
   preferences for each channel is its own data and UI problem.

4. An alert triggering/routing layer - something must decide when an alert fires
   and which channels to invoke for which user.

The word "and" appears between three delivery channels, which is the clearest
signal that the story contains parallel tracks that can be delivered independently.
"Automatically" and "real-time" add cross-cutting quality constraints that are
often better validated incrementally rather than all at once.

Attempting to build and test all of this in a single iteration would likely result
in a story that takes several weeks, is hard to demo incrementally, and has many
integration points that make debugging slow.


PROPOSED SPLIT
--------------

Story 1 - Notification preference model
  "As a user, I can set my notification preferences (email on/off, SMS on/off,
  in-app on/off) so the system knows which channels to use when alerting me."

  Scope: data model, API to read/write preferences, basic UI to toggle channels.
  No actual sending required. Acceptance: preferences are persisted and returned
  correctly via API.

Story 2 - In-app notifications (synchronous baseline)
  "As a user, I receive in-app alerts when a relevant event occurs so I can see
  notifications without leaving the application."

  Scope: notification record creation, in-app feed or badge counter, no real-time
  push yet (polling is acceptable). Filters by preferences from Story 1.
  Acceptance: notification appears in the UI after the triggering event.

Story 3 - Real-time delivery for in-app notifications
  "As a user, in-app alerts appear immediately without me having to refresh the
  page so I get timely information."

  Scope: upgrade the in-app channel from polling to WebSocket / SSE push.
  Acceptance: notification appears within 1-2 seconds of the event without a
  page reload.

Story 4 - Email notifications
  "As a user, I receive an email alert when a relevant event occurs and my email
  notifications are enabled so I can be notified even when I am not in the app."

  Scope: integrate email service (e.g. SendGrid/SES), templating, delivery,
  bounce/error handling. Respects preference from Story 1.
  Acceptance: email is received within a reasonable time; no email sent when
  preference is disabled.

Story 5 - SMS notifications
  "As a user, I receive an SMS alert when a relevant event occurs and my SMS
  notifications are enabled so I am reachable on my phone."

  Scope: integrate SMS provider (e.g. Twilio), message formatting, opt-out
  compliance (STOP keyword). Respects preference from Story 1.
  Acceptance: SMS received when enabled; not sent when disabled; opt-out works.


DELIVERY ORDER
--------------

Recommended sequence: 1 -> 2 -> 4 -> 5 -> 3

- Story 1 unblocks everything else (preferences must exist first).
- Story 2 delivers the core value (users see alerts) with minimal infrastructure.
- Stories 4 and 5 add channels independently; either can be skipped or deferred.
- Story 3 is a quality/performance upgrade that can wait until the baseline is
  validated with real users; polling in Story 2 is a safe starting point.

Each story in this split is independently deployable, demonstrable to
stakeholders, and testable in isolation.
